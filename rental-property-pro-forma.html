RENTAL PROPERTY ANALYSIS

Rental property pro forma: example, inputs and reports

A pro forma is a projection of a property's income, expenses and financial performance. It helps you test a proposed purchase before committing to an offer; it is not a record of guaranteed future results.

What should a rental property pro forma include?

  • Acquisition: purchase price, initial repairs, closing costs and cash invested.
  • Income: rent per unit, number of units, vacancy and assumptions about rent growth.
  • Operating expenses: property taxes, insurance, maintenance, management and owner-paid costs.
  • Financing: loan amount, interest rate, amortization, fees and any interest-only period.
  • Holding period and exit: projected sale price, selling costs, remaining debt and modeled taxes.

Keep documented facts separate from estimates. Record the source and date of rent and expense assumptions so someone reviewing the report can challenge them.

A simple annual cash flow example

Consider a hypothetical one-unit rental with monthly rent of $2,000. This example illustrates operating cash flow only, before income taxes, capital improvements and sale proceeds.

Scheduled annual rent: $2,000 × 12 = $24,000Vacancy allowance: $24,000 × 5% = $1,200Effective rental income: $24,000 − $1,200 = $22,800Operating expenses: $8,000Net operating income: $22,800 − $8,000 = $14,800Annual debt service: $12,000Before-tax cash flow: $14,800 − $12,000 = $2,800

The equivalent monthly average is about $233, but actual cash receipts and repairs will not arrive evenly. Positive operating cash flow alone does not establish that the acquisition price is reasonable or that the investment meets your required return.

How agents can present an analysis to investor clients

Explain the source of the rent estimate, which expenses need confirmation and how the financing assumptions were selected. Present a downside scenario as well as the base case. A report supports a discussion about an offer; it does not replace an appraisal, inspection or professional tax advice.

Check the assumptions before relying on the report

  • Confirm that mortgage payments have not also been included in operating expenses.
  • Include vacancy and repairs instead of assuming every rent payment becomes spendable cash.
  • Compare loan payoff with the amortization schedule, particularly for interest-only loans.
  • Distinguish first-year cash-on-cash return from holding-period IRR.
  • Recalculate after changing assumptions, then export a fresh report.
