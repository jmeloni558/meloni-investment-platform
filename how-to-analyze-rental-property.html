RENTAL PROPERTY ANALYSIS GUIDE

How to analyze a rental property before you buy

A rental property analysis tests whether expected income supports operating costs, financing, investor returns and the proposed price. Use this seven-step workflow to turn a promising listing into an evidence-based investment decision.

The analysis sequence
  1. Define the acquisition
  2. Estimate sustainable income
  3. Budget operating expenses
  4. Calculate NOI and cap rate
  5. Model financing and cash flow
  6. Evaluate long-term returns
  7. Test value, risk and offer price
STEP 1

Define the acquisition and property

Begin with purchase price, address, unit count, closing costs and immediate repairs. Keep observable property facts separate from estimates. Price affects the loan, equity required, cap rate, cash-on-cash return and every value comparison that follows.

STEP 2

Estimate sustainable rental income

Use rent supported by leases and comparable rentals, adjusted for condition, amenities, utilities and concessions. Potential Gross Income assumes full occupancy. Effective Gross Income recognizes vacancy and collection loss.

PGI Monthly rent × units × 12EGI PGI − vacancy and credit loss + other income
STEP 3

Build a complete operating-expense budget

Include property taxes, insurance, management, maintenance, owner-paid utilities, association dues and replacement reserves. Do not count mortgage payments or income taxes as operating expenses; those are modeled separately. Replace percentage defaults with property-specific estimates as evidence becomes available.

STEP 4

Calculate NOI and cap rate

Net Operating Income measures property operations before financing and income taxes. Cap rate compares that income with price without regard to the loan structure.

NOI Effective Gross Income − operating expensesCap rate Year 1 NOI ÷ acquisition price

A cap rate is useful for comparison, but it excludes financing, future growth, sale proceeds and tax effects.

STEP 5

Model financing, DSCR and cash flow

Enter the loan amount, rate, amortization, points and origination fees. Annual debt service reduces cash flow, while principal reduction builds equity. Debt Service Coverage Ratio shows whether NOI covers scheduled debt payments.

DSCR NOI ÷ annual debt serviceBefore-tax cash flow NOI − debt serviceCash-on-cash return Annual cash flow ÷ initial cash invested
STEP 6

Evaluate the holding period and exit

Project rent growth, expenses, appreciation, loan balance, selling costs and taxes across the expected hold. IRR reflects the timing of cash flows and sale proceeds. NPV measures whether projected cash flows exceed the investor’s required return in present-value terms. These outputs depend on the assumptions, so conservative evidence matters more than a precise-looking percentage.

STEP 7

Reconcile value, risk and offer strategy

Compare price with income-supported value, gross-rent-multiplier evidence and relevant comparable sales. Stress-test lower rent, higher vacancy, increased expenses, interest-rate changes and a weaker exit. A defensible investment thesis states what must be true, what evidence supports it and which risks could invalidate it.
