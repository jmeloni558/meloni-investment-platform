PROPERTY INVESTMENT, EXPLAINED

Understand every input. Read every result with confidence.

Use this guide while building an analysis or reviewing a PropertyThesis report. It covers every input in the original investment calculator, the detailed expense options, and the metrics the analysis produces.

Property & incomeExpensesFinancingTax & exitReturn targetsCalculated metricsEvidence & reports
Inputs are assumptions you enter or revise. Metrics are calculated from those assumptions. PropertyThesis defaults are visible starting points—not property-specific facts—and should be replaced with actual or market-supported information when available.
01
CALCULATOR INPUTS

Property & income

Property / Analysis Name

A label used to identify the property and its saved analysis. It does not affect calculations.

Property Address

The location shown in the analysis and report and used to connect available property and market research.

Acquisition Price

The proposed purchase price used in financing, initial equity, cap-rate, return, and value comparisons.

Land Value

The portion of the acquisition attributed to land. Land is not depreciated, so it is excluded from depreciable basis.

Number of Units / Apartments

The rentable unit count. Average rent per unit is multiplied by this number when calculating potential income.

Monthly Rent

Expected monthly rent for a single-unit property, or average monthly rent per unit for a multi-unit property.

Annual Rent Growth

The annual percentage change in rent beginning in Year 2, compounded through the holding period.

Vacancy & Credit Loss

The percentage of potential gross income deducted for vacancy, turnover, concessions, and uncollected rent.

Expected Holding Period

The number of years—between 1 and 40—the property is expected to be held before sale.

02
CALCULATOR INPUTS

Operating expenses

Choose either method: enter a quick expense ratio, or build a detailed annual budget. Mortgage payments and income taxes are modeled separately.

Operating Expenses as % of EGI

A quick estimate of recurring operating costs as a percentage of Effective Gross Income. The visible starting default is 40%.

Property Taxes

Annual real estate taxes charged by local authorities. Use the expected post-purchase amount when reassessment is likely.

Insurance

Annual property and liability premiums, including relevant hazard or flood coverage.

Management

Annual property-management expense, including an economic allowance when the owner self-manages.

Repairs & Maintenance

Recurring costs to keep the property operating in its current condition. Major improvements are separate.

Owner-Paid Utilities

Electricity, water, sewer, trash, gas, internet, or other utilities paid by the owner.

HOA / Association

Recurring homeowners’, condominium, or community association dues paid by the owner.

Other Expenses / Reserves

Other recurring costs and reserves for future replacement of roofs, HVAC, appliances, paving, and similar items.

03
CALCULATOR INPUTS

Financing

Mortgage Amount

The principal borrowed to acquire the property. Enter zero for an all-cash purchase.

Loan Structure

Amortizing payments reduce principal. Interest only payments do not reduce scheduled principal during the modeled term.

Mortgage Rate

The annual interest rate charged on the outstanding loan balance.

Mortgage Time Horizon

The years over which payments are calculated. A longer amortization generally lowers payments but slows principal reduction.

Mortgage Points

Upfront lender charges stated as a percentage of the mortgage. One point equals 1% of the loan.

Origination Fee

A separate dollar fee charged to originate the loan, increasing initial cash required.

04
CALCULATOR INPUTS

Taxes, growth & exit

Depreciable Life

The recovery period used to depreciate the building portion. Land is excluded; tax treatment varies.

Annual Property Appreciation

The annual percentage change in property value after Year 1, compounded through the sale year.

Selling Expenses

The percentage of projected sale price deducted for brokerage, title, closing, transfer, and other sale costs.

Ordinary Income Tax Rate

The estimated marginal rate applied to modeled taxable operating income.

Depreciation Tax Rate

The estimated rate applied to the depreciation-related portion of gain at sale.

Capital Gains Tax Rate

The estimated rate applied to modeled long-term appreciation gain after separating depreciation-related gain.

05
CALCULATOR INPUTS

Return & valuation targets

Required Rate of Return

The investor’s minimum annual return target and the discount rate used to calculate NPV.

Desired Capitalization Rate

The target cap rate used to convert Year 1 NOI into an indicated income-supported value.

Desired Gross Rent Multiplier

The target ratio of property value to annual potential gross rent.

Comparable Sales Selection

Optional properties chosen as market evidence. Relevance depends on similarity, timing, condition, and transaction status.

06
CALCULATED OUTPUTS

Income, financing, value & returns

Potential Gross Income (PGI)

Total scheduled rental income at full occupancy before vacancy and credit loss.

Monthly rent × units × 12

Effective Gross Income (EGI)

Expected income after vacancy and credit loss are deducted.

PGI − vacancy and credit loss

Net Operating Income (NOI)

Income after operating expenses but before mortgage payments, depreciation, capital expenditures, and income taxes.

EGI − operating expenses

Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)

Year 1 NOI as a percentage of price or value; an unlevered income-yield measure.

NOI ÷ price or value

Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)

Price relative to annual potential gross rent. It does not account for vacancy or expenses.

Price ÷ annual gross rent

Debt Service

Total scheduled principal and interest payments during a period.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

NOI relative to debt payments. Above 1.00x means modeled NOI exceeds debt service.

NOI ÷ annual debt service

Loan-to-Value (LTV)

Mortgage amount as a percentage of property price or value.

Loan amount ÷ property value

Initial Equity

The modeled cash invested at acquisition after debt and applicable upfront financing costs.

Before-Tax Cash Flow

Operating cash remaining after debt service but before income taxes.

NOI − debt service

After-Tax Cash Flow

Modeled operating cash flow after debt service and estimated income-tax effects.

Cash-on-Cash Return

Annual cash flow relative to initial cash equity; it excludes unrealized appreciation.

Annual cash flow ÷ initial equity

Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

The annualized discount rate at which modeled inflows equal the initial investment, including sale proceeds.

Net Present Value (NPV)

Present value of future cash flows minus initial investment, discounted at the required return. Positive NPV exceeds that target.

Equity Multiple

Total modeled cash distributions divided by total equity invested; it does not measure timing.

Principal Reduction

The decrease in loan balance from amortization, which builds equity but is not current cash flow.

Taxable Income

Modeled income after allowable deductions such as interest and depreciation; it is not the same as cash flow.

Depreciation

A non-cash tax deduction allocating depreciable building basis over the recovery period.

Projected Sale Price

The modeled value in the sale year after applying annual appreciation.

Net Sale Proceeds / Equity Reversion

Sale cash after selling expenses, loan payoff, and modeled sale taxes.

Income-Supported Value

Indicated value derived from NOI and the desired cap rate.

NOI ÷ desired cap rate

GRM-Supported Value

Indicated value derived from annual potential gross rent and desired GRM.

Annual gross rent × desired GRM

Maximum Supported Price

The highest modeled acquisition price satisfying the selected return target or valuation conclusion.

07
DECISION & DELIVERABLE TERMS

Evidence, analysis & reports

Property Thesis

The investment premise being tested: that the property can produce the expected income, value, returns, and risk profile.

Market Rent

Rent reasonably supported by the market rather than only the current lease.

Rental Comparable

A similar rental used to support market rent, considering location, size, condition, features, and timing.

Sales Comparable

A similar sold or listed property used as pricing evidence.

Sensitivity Analysis

Shows how results change when assumptions such as price, rent, vacancy, expenses, or rate change.

Pro Forma

A multiyear projection of income, expenses, financing, taxes, cash flow, value, and sale proceeds.

Professional Report

A client-ready presentation of the property, assumptions, performance, valuation evidence, returns, risks, and conclusion.

Assumption Quality

How well key inputs are supported by property-specific or market evidence, highlighting items still needing verification.
